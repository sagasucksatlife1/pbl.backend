The Problem

Traditional portfolio optimization models like Markowitz assume:

  • Linear relationships between assets
  • Independent variables with no complex interactions
  • Static behavior across different market conditions

But real financial markets are complex and nonlinear.

Model Performance Analysis

Random Forest: Excellent Performance (R² ≈ 0.97)

The Random Forest model achieves an R² score of approximately 0.97, demonstrating exceptional predictive accuracy. This success proves a critical hypothesis: financial market risk exhibits strong non-linear relationships between variables.

Random Forest excels at capturing complex interactions because it:

  • Creates decision trees that naturally model non-linear relationships
  • Captures interaction effects between features (e.g., how volatility and beta combine to affect risk)
  • Handles complex patterns without assuming linear relationships
  • Aggregates predictions from 100 trees, reducing overfitting while maintaining flexibility

Neural Network: Challenges and Solutions

The Neural Network shows poor performance (negative R²), which is common in financial modeling with limited data. This doesn't invalidate the approach—it highlights important considerations:

Why Neural Networks Struggle Here:
  • Insufficient Training Data: Neural networks require thousands of samples to learn effectively. Our dataset has only 1,000 samples—adequate for Random Forest but insufficient for deep learning.
  • High Dimensionality vs. Sample Size: With 8 features and 3 hidden layers, the network has many parameters relative to available data, leading to overfitting.
  • Hyperparameter Sensitivity: Learning rate, batch size, and architecture choices critically affect performance and require extensive tuning.
  • Feature Scaling Issues: Neural networks are sensitive to feature scales. Variables like volume (millions) and returns (decimals) exist on vastly different scales.
How to Improve Neural Network Performance:
  • Collect More Data: Increase training samples to 10,000+ observations to give the network sufficient examples to learn from.
  • Feature Engineering: Apply standardization (z-score normalization) so all features have mean=0 and std=1.
  • Simplify Architecture: Reduce to 2 layers (e.g., 32-16) to decrease parameters and reduce overfitting risk.
  • Regularization: Add L2 regularization and dropout layers to prevent the model from memorizing training data.
  • Early Stopping: Monitor validation loss and stop training when it stops improving.
  • Learning Rate Tuning: Use adaptive learning rates (Adam optimizer with learning rate scheduling).
  • Ensemble Approach: Combine Random Forest (which works) with an improved Neural Network for robust predictions.

Key Insight: Non-Linearity in Financial Markets

The stark performance difference validates our core thesis: traditional linear models miss critical relationships in financial data. Random Forest's success demonstrates that variable interactions (volatility × beta, momentum × market conditions) drive portfolio risk more than individual variables acting independently. This finding has direct implications for real-world portfolio construction and risk management.
